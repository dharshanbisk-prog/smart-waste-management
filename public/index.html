System Architecture & EVS Documentation

IoT-Enabled Smart Waste Monitoring and Route Optimization

1. Environmental Studies (EVS) Problem Context

Traditional municipal solid waste collection operates on rigid, predetermined daily schedules. Sanitation trucks visit empty bins unnecessarily (burning excess diesel and emitting greenhouse gases) while overflowing bins remain uncollected, triggering foul odors, disease vectors (flies, rats), leachate runoff into stormwater drains, and plastic contamination in urban ecosystems.

2. Hardware & IoT Sensor Stack

Ultrasonic Sensor HC-SR04 / JSN-SR04T waterproof sensor installed under the bin lid. Emits 40 kHz ultrasonic pulses to measure the distance to the trash surface.
Microcontroller ESP32 dual-core microcontroller with integrated Wi-Fi / LoRaWAN transceiver. Operates in deep sleep mode to conserve energy.
Power Harvest 3.7V 2600mAh 18650 Li-ion battery paired with a 5V 1W top-lid solar photovoltaic harvester for autonomous off-grid operation.

3. Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP) Optimization

Instead of executing a fixed route visiting all bins, the system dynamically filters for bins exceeding the critical threshold (>80% capacity). Using great-circle Haversine distance computations and a Nearest-Neighbor TSP heuristic, it calculates the shortest Hamiltonian cycle departing from and returning to the Central Municipal Depot.

Haversine Formula: d = 2R × arcsin(√[sin²(Δφ/2) + cos(φ₁)cos(φ₂)sin²(Δλ/2)])
Diesel CO2e Factor: 2.68 kg CO2e / Liter Diesel @ 0.34 L / km

4. Quantified Sustainability Outcomes

  • 35% to 50% reduction in total sanitation truck travel distance.
  • Reduced Fuel Consumption: Saves approx. 1.5 to 2.5 Liters of diesel per pickup run.
  • Greenhouse Gas Reduction: Prevents ~4 to 7 kg of CO2e and NOx pollutants per collection trip.
  • Zero Overflow Spills: Rapid targeted dispatch prevents hazardous municipal runoff.
